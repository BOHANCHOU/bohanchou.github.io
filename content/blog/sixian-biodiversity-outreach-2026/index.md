On 22 May 2026, the International Day for Biological Diversity, I joined the team from the School of Resources and Environmental Engineering, Anhui University, for a biodiversity science outreach program in Sixian County, Anhui, China. The activity was organized by the Sixian Ecological Environment Sub-bureau and brought biodiversity education into local schools, including Mingde Primary School and Xiaqiu Central School.

The outreach drew on findings from local biodiversity baseline surveys and focused on the theme **“Protect Local Life, Let All Things Thrive Together.”** During the lecture, I introduced the three levels of biodiversity—genetic diversity, species diversity, and ecosystem diversity—through accessible analogies designed for school students. We also discussed the importance of biodiversity conservation, representative local species, and the prevention and management of invasive species.

Rather than presenting biodiversity as an abstract concept, the session combined local ecological examples, field photographs, quizzes, interactive activities, and case-based explanations. This helped connect biodiversity survey work with environmental education and gave students a more direct understanding of the relationships among species, ecosystems, and human activities.

The activity was attended by more than one hundred teachers and students. For me, it was also an opportunity to extend biodiversity fieldwork beyond surveys and reports by translating ecological knowledge into public-facing science communication.
